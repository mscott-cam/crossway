Three more directions, more serious and established

These replace the first round. They drop the playful shapes and the technical grid, and aim for a church that feels established and trustworthy while staying warm. Navy, amber, the CW mark and the live site's content carry over. Items marked "to confirm" are placeholders, and the muted blue panels show where the church's own photos go.

Idea

A building's dedication stone, made into a website. Calm, centred and symmetrical, with lettering that looks cut into limestone and a thin amber line inlaid like brass. One wide photograph carries the warmth and the text stays quiet.

Look

NavyAmberLimestoneShadow

Marcellus for headings, Libre Caslon Text for reading.

Motion

The photograph settles in slowly. The amber lines draw across once and each section rises a few pixels into place. Nothing else moves.

Risk

The most formal. It leans on strong photography, and the soft stone tones need careful contrast so older readers are comfortable. The centred layout limits how dense inner pages can get.

Idea

The trust of a good hospital or university. A large photograph of the congregation, a navy panel with a plain headline, and an information bar that answers when, where and how to watch before anyone scrolls. Strong hierarchy, no ornament.

Look

NavyAmberWhiteMist

Libre Franklin for headings, Source Serif 4 for reading.

Motion

The photograph is revealed upward like a curtain, then the headline and information bar come in. The photo drifts a few pixels as you scroll.

Risk

The safest and closest to what other churches ship, so it stands out only through execution and real photography. Without good photos it can look like a template.

Idea

A navy cloth-bound book stamped in gold. The opening is a foil-framed title page, the sections read like chapters, and events and sermons are set like a table of contents with dotted leaders.

Look

Navy clothAmber foilPaperWhite

Old Standard TT for headings, Spectral for reading.

Motion

The foil frame is stamped across the opening page, a bookmark ribbon drops in, and the title fades up. Contents lines extend as you reach them.

Risk

The most traditional and ornamental. It signals heritage strongly, which may feel old-fashioned to younger visitors, and it has to avoid costume details such as page curls or fake leather.

Scroll inside each mockup to see the page motion. Motion is off when your system asks for reduced motion.
