Three more directions, modern with an old-church feel

These build on Option 2's trusted, photo-led feel but drop the template layout. They use curves, arches and hand-drawn flourishes in place of boxes and cards. Navy, amber, the CW mark and the live site's content carry over. Items marked "to confirm" are placeholders, and the blue panels show where the church's own photos go.

Idea

Pointed-arch windows, the shape of an old church's stained glass, used as photo frames with hairline arches echoing outside them. A flowing path replaces boxed cards, and the headline carries a hand-drawn swash.

Look

NavyAmberPaperMist

Sorts Mill Goudy for headings and reading, Hanken Grotesk for navigation and buttons.

Motion

The arch outlines draw themselves, the photo rises into its window and a swash underlines "loved." Further down the windows rise and the path between the three steps draws as you scroll.

Risk

Gothic arches can turn theatrical if overused, so they stay on photos and one triptych. The airy look depends on good photography and generous space.

Idea

A rose window as the centrepiece. A circular photo sits inside amber tracery, sections meet in large curves instead of straight edges, and the photo band opens from a circle as you scroll. Deep navy and amber give the feel of light through glass.

Look

Deep navyNavyAmberPaper

Alegreya for headings and reading, Cinzel Decorative for the church name only.

Motion

The window tracery draws itself and the photo settles inside it. The photo band opens from a circle as you scroll, and each step's ring draws when you reach it.

Risk

The most dramatic and the darkest. The circles and curves need discipline to stay dignified, and the decorative capitals must stay limited to the name to avoid a fantasy look.

Idea

A full-bleed photo under a swash of calligraphy, rising into the page through a gothic arch. First Sunday details are written as a letter with the key facts underlined by hand, and thin vines mark the transitions, like an illuminated manuscript made modern.

Look

NavyAmberPaperDeep navy

Brygada 1918 for headings and reading, Pinyon Script for the word "loved." and the footer name only.

Motion

The word "loved." writes itself left to right, a vine grows beneath it and the arch rises into place. Underlines draw across the key facts as you scroll.

Risk

The most ornamental, closest to formal stationery. Script lettering has to stay rare to keep everything readable, and the key facts must stay plain and legible beyond the decoration.
